# Airxpact Aviation Glossary for Travellers

**IATA code** - three-letter code used on tickets and boarding passes for airports (LOS = Lagos Murtala Muhammed, LHR = London Heathrow, DXB = Dubai) and two-letter codes for airlines (BA = British Airways, ET = Ethiopian Airlines).
**ICAO code** - four-letter code used by pilots, air traffic control and weather reports (DNMM = Lagos, EGLL = London Heathrow, OMDB = Dubai, HAAB = Addis Ababa, LTFM = Istanbul, OTHH = Doha Hamad).
**Layover / connection** - time spent at an intermediate airport between two flights. A layover over 24 hours on an international trip is usually called a stopover.
**Minimum connection time (MCT)** - the shortest connection an airline or airport will sell. MCTs are set per airport and terminal. As a rule of thumb, under 60 minutes on a domestic connection or under 90 minutes on an international connection is risky, and more time is needed if you must change terminals, clear immigration or recheck bags.
**Protected connection** - both flights are on one ticket, so the airline must rebook you if the first flight is late. Separate tickets ("self-transfer") are not protected.
**Codeshare** - one flight sold under two or more airlines' flight numbers. The operating carrier (the one flying the plane) sets check-in, baggage on the day and boarding rules.
**Interline** - an agreement that lets airlines check bags through and issue one ticket across their flights.
**Airline alliances** - Star Alliance, oneworld and SkyTeam. Members share lounges, status benefits and often connections.
**Fare class / booking class** - the letter on your ticket (e.g. Y, B, M, Q) that sets the price level and rules inside a cabin.
**Fare brand** - the marketed bundle (Basic, Classic, Flex) that tells you what is included: baggage, seat selection, changes and refunds.
**Basic economy** - cheapest fare; often no free seat choice, no changes, no refunds, sometimes no checked bag.
**Flexible economy** - higher price; changes allowed with low or no fee, often refundable.
**Carry-on / cabin bag** - bag you take into the cabin. Most airlines allow one cabin bag of about 7-10 kg plus a small personal item. Limits vary by airline and fare.
**Checked baggage** - bags given at check-in and carried in the hold. Allowances are by piece (e.g. 2 x 23 kg) or by weight.
**PNR / booking reference** - six-character code (e.g. X7K2LM) that identifies your booking.
**E-ticket number** - 13-digit number; the first three digits identify the issuing airline.
**Boarding group / zone** - the order in which passengers board. Lower numbers board first.
**Gate closes** - the time after which you will not be allowed to board, usually 15-20 minutes before departure.
**Overbooking / denied boarding** - airlines sell slightly more seats than exist; if too many passengers show up, some may be denied boarding and are usually entitled to compensation under local passenger-rights rules.
**Flight level (FL)** - altitude in hundreds of feet based on standard pressure. FL350 is about 35,000 ft. Pilots announce it so passengers know cruising altitude.
**Turbulence** - irregular air movement. It is uncomfortable but modern aircraft are built to handle it. Keeping your seatbelt fastened when seated is the best protection.
**A320 vs Boeing 737** - both are single-aisle, short-to-medium-haul jets seating about 150-200 passengers. The A320 family (Airbus) uses fly-by-wire with side-sticks; the 737 (Boeing) uses a traditional control column. Passengers notice small differences in cabin width (the A320 cabin is slightly wider).
**METAR** - routine airport weather observation, usually issued every 30-60 minutes.
**TAF** - Terminal Aerodrome Forecast, the official airport forecast covering 24-30 hours.
